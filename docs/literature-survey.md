# Literature and software survey for a GKL replacement

Scope: CPU acceleration of the GATK HaplotypeCaller PairHMM and Smith-Waterman kernels, plus JVM-to-native call overhead. Compiled September 2026. Each entry gives citation, technique, reported numbers with their baseline, and a verdict (adopt / consider / not applicable) for the planned design: inter-task SIMD lanes (reads against one haplotype), row-major sweep, DP-column snapshots at LCP boundaries between sorted haplotypes, f32 with f64 fallback, AVX2/AVX-512/NEON dispatch, Rust. Vendor and pipeline-level numbers are flagged as unverified where I could not check them.

## 1. PairHMM

### Implementations and measured performance

- **Intel GKL** (IntelLabs, 2016-2025, archived; https://github.com/IntelLabs/GKL). Intra-task anti-diagonal vectorization of one read x haplotype matrix (8 f32 lanes AVX, 16 AVX-512), f32 first with f64 recomputation when the result is below `MIN_ACCEPTED` (1e-28), `omp parallel for schedule(dynamic,1)` over pairs, per-pair recomputation of per-read tables, FTZ enabled. Independently measured (gpuPairHMM, below) on the "10s" dataset (3,550 pairs, mean read length 55): 1.58 ms with AVX2 on 128 threads, 1.74 ms with AVX-512 on 88 threads, versus 10.8 s for the sequential log-space Java baseline. Verdict: the baseline to beat; anti-diagonals waste lanes on the ramp of every 150-row matrix and spend instructions on cross-lane shifts, which inter-task lanes avoid.

- **GATK Java `PairHMM` / `LoglessPairHMM`** (https://github.com/broadinstitute/gatk/blob/master/src/main/java/org/broadinstitute/hellbender/utils/pairhmm/PairHMM.java). The Java path already exploits shared haplotype prefixes: `findFirstPositionWhereHaplotypesDiffer` yields `hapStartIndex` and the DP restarts at that column ("starting only at the place where the new haplotype bases and the previous haplotype bases differ"); `recacheReadValues=false` reuses per-read transition/emission tables across haplotypes. The GKL path (`VectorLoglessPairHMM`) sends all pairs to native and drops both. Verdict: **adopt**. Direct, exact precedent for the LCP-snapshot design; GATK does not sort haplotypes to maximize LCP, so sorting is a small genuine improvement.

- **Endeavor** (Graça & Ilic, HPDC 2026; arXiv:2606.25738). Eliminates the D matrix (expressed as a scan over M on the previous row), giving row-level parallelism in two passes per read position. CPU version: one pair per SIMD lane (inter-task), AVX2/AVX-512, FP32/FP64. Reports up to 2.14x peak throughput over GKL AVX-512 (Xeon Gold 6438), 10.6x average over GKL on AMD Zen 4 (suspicious; probably GKL's dispatch on AMD rather than kernel quality), and "2.29x-25.32x over GATK HaplotypeCaller" on GIAB data (unclear whether end-to-end; unverified). Claims bit-identical output. No code found. Verdict: **consider as a calibration point**: their CPU kernel is essentially the planned layout, so ~2x over GKL is the floor before prefix sharing. The D-elimination trick removes an intra-task dependency our layout does not have and adds a per-row scan; do not adopt.

- **Rovaca** (Zheng & Yin, bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.10.19.677660, Oct 2025; https://github.com/ZephyRoy/Rovaca, MIT, C++, x86 AVX2+). Full HC rewrite. PairHMM packs 16 reads of near-identical length (+/-5 bp windows) per AVX-512 f32 vector so no lane masking is needed, and reverses data on load so lane extraction hits cheap low-index positions. Reports 57-76x over GATK 4.6 on WGS, ~30x WES, F1 within 0.01% (whole-caller, multi-threaded; not a kernel number). Verdict: **adopt the length binning**; read its PairHMM source before designing the batch scheduler.

- **gpuPairHMM** (Schmidt, Kallenborn, Wichmann, Chacon, Hundt; arXiv:2411.11547, 2024; journal 2025, PMID 41576120; https://github.com/asbschmidt/gpuPairHMM). Sub-warp per pair with warp-shuffle wavefronts, kernels specialized per read-length bin, f32 only. Precomputes each read's emission probabilities as a 5 x n table (one entry per possible haplotype base) instead of per cell. Peak 2.62 TCUPS (L40S), 1.82 TCUPS on real NA12878 batches including transfers; 10s dataset 0.118-0.152 ms vs GKL 1.58 ms. Claims identical scores to GATK, but f32 without rescaling cannot reproduce GATK's double fallback on underflowing pairs, so read as "within tolerance on tested data". Verdict: **adopt** the 5 x n emission table (a gather per (row, haplotype base) replaces a compare-and-select per cell) and length binning.

- **FPGA line**: Huang et al. (FPGA 2017, doi:10.1145/3020078.3021749; ring systolic array), Sampietro et al. (ASAP 2018), Wang, Lei, Dou (IEICE Electronics Express 2019), Wertenbroek & Thoma (BIBM 2019; AWS F1; https://github.com/rick-heig/PHMM-F1; 2160x over Java, and after acceleration the remaining Java stages dominate), Ito & Ohara (2016, POWER8 CAPI systolic array, 33x power-performance vs CPU; venue unverified). I could not verify a separate Banerjee et al. PairHMM paper. On the same 10s dataset these run 2.2-8.3 ms, i.e. 1.3-5x slower than GKL on a two-socket CPU (gpuPairHMM Table II). Ren, Bertels, Al-Ars (Evol. Bioinformatics 2018) is GPU-only and older. Verdict: not applicable, except that a systolic PE array streaming one haplotype against many reads is the planned inter-task dataflow.

- **Closed vendors**. Illumina DRAGEN (Edico patent US10068054B2: FPGA HMM engines with programmable-width floating point, ~100 jobs per 4 KB page; "HC 9 h to 23 min" is pipeline-level and unverified). NVIDIA Parabricks (closed; "36 h to ~30 min" pipeline, unverified; Broad-verified functional equivalence; some paths fall back to AVX-512 CPU PairHMM). Sentieon (closed; Kendig et al., Front. Genet. 2019: ~10x core-hour efficiency for the whole pipeline; no published PairHMM technique). Verdict: not applicable; nothing technical to reuse.

- **Octopus** (Cooke, Wedge, Lunter, Nat. Biotech. 2021; `src/core/models/pairhmm`): banded Viterbi (max, not sum) in phred integers, intra-task anti-diagonal, SSE2/AVX2/AVX-512/NEON. **Strelka2** (Kim et al., Nat. Methods 2018): max over a few enumerated candidate alignments instead of the full sum. Verdict: not applicable (different models); Octopus is a usable reference NEON anti-diagonal kernel.

- **LPA** (BMC Bioinformatics 2025, doi:10.1186/s12859-025-06242-w; https://github.com/laixx9/LPA-dataset). Predicts per-region cost from variant count, read count and depth and schedules regions across processes via multi-knapsack; 30-40x over single-process HC, 1.3-2x over similar schedulers. Verdict: **consider** the cost model for ordering batches under dynamic thread scheduling.

- **Broad/Intel PDHMM, 2025** (https://gatk.broadinstitute.org/hc/en-us/articles/41565974956955). GKL gained an AVX-512 Partially-Determined HMM (`--dragen-pd-pair-hmm-implementation AVX_LOGLESS_CACHING`): >6x single-core over Java PDHMM, up to 3.9x on DRAGEN-mode HC, where PDHMM was 75-85% of runtime. Verdict: **scope**: a full GKL replacement needs PDHMM or DRAGEN-mode users fall back to slow Java.

- **Rust and ARM ecosystem**. The `gkl` crate (philipc, 0.1.1, 2026) is a `cc`-built binding to Intel's C++ (x86 only). gatk-rs (https://github.com/SynapticFour/gatk-rs, AI-authored, alpha) has NEON microbenchmarks it labels non-publishable. No NEON/SVE GKL port exists; GATK on aarch64 falls back to Java. Graviton 3 exposes 256-bit SVE; Graviton 4 and Apple silicon are 128-bit. Verdict: nothing reusable; with 4 f32 lanes per NEON register, run two to four independent lane groups per loop iteration to fill the pipeline, so design around "lanes per group" not "lanes per register".

### Numerics and skipping work

- **Scaled forward algorithm** (Rabiner, Proc. IEEE 1989) and **HMMER3 sparse rescaling** (Eddy, PLoS Comput. Biol. 2011, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002195). HMMER3 keeps Forward in probability space with f32 lanes and rescales a row only when a sentinel cell crosses a threshold; its local multihit model guarantees long deletion runs never matter, which is what makes sparse rescaling safe. Verdict: **adopt in modified form**. GATK's model is global in the read, so rescale per lane per row by a power of two chosen from the row maximum, accumulating the exponent in an integer lane. Power-of-two scaling is exact in IEEE arithmetic, so results are bit-identical to unscaled f32 wherever unscaled f32 did not underflow, and the f64 recompute disappears. Caveat: on pairs where GKL would fall back to f64, our f32 answer differs from GKL's at ~1e-7 relative, the same order as GKL-f32 vs GATK-Java-f64 today; keep an f64 path for parity tests.

- **GATK downstream likelihood cap** (`PairHMMLikelihoodCalculationEngine.normalizeLikelihoods`; `--phred-scaled-global-read-mismapping-rate`, default 45). Each read's likelihoods are clamped to at most 10^-4.5 below its best haplotype before genotyping. Verdict: **consider (benchmark only)**. A pair whose sound upper bound is below best - 4.5 log10 can be reported as the bound with zero downstream effect, but this needs the best haplotype first and a cheap bound; the exactness argument is against the Java normalization, not the kernel.

- **Pruning-based Pair-HMM ASIC** (Wu, Subramaniyan, Wang, Narayanasamy, Das, Blaauw; IEEE Solid-State Circuits Letters 2021, doi:10.1109/LSSC.2020.3045148). A 20-bit log-domain fixed-point scan bounds each matrix, prunes 97.7% of cells from FP refinement, and recomputes when a genotype-level bound check fails; 43x less FP work, 17.3 GCUPS in 40 nm. Verdict: **consider** only as the bound mechanism for the cap idea; as an approximation, not applicable.

- **Banded Pair-HMM** (Chen et al., IEEE 2019, ieeexplore document 8941870; paywalled). Heuristic; accuracy tradeoffs unverifiable from the abstract. Verdict: not applicable while exact GATK equivalence is required.

- **fp16 / bf16**. No published PairHMM work found. My assessment: fp16's 2^-14 normal range cannot hold within-row dynamic range (M cell vs a long gap run routinely exceeds 2^-24) even with per-row rescaling; bf16's 8-bit mantissa gives ~0.4% error per operation compounding across ~10^5 cells. Verdict: not applicable.

- **Log-space**. GATK's move to linear "logless" space with the 2^1020 / 2^120 initial constant (Carneiro, 2013-2014, https://github.com/MauricioCarneiro/PairHMM) is what made SIMD multiply-add feasible. Verdict: keep linear space.

- **Prefix/suffix sharing**. Trie-based prefix reuse is standard in genotype-likelihood HMMs (GEDI; Kennedy, Mandoiu, Pasaniuc 2009), but no PairHMM implementation other than GATK's Java path does it. Suffix sharing is available by reversing both read and haplotype (same path set and products; the per-read-position transition table reverses with the read), but summation order changes, so it is not bit-identical. Verdict: **adopt prefix sharing**; measure LCP / haplotype-length distributions on real regions first since the win is bounded by that ratio; keep reversal as an optional experiment.

## 2. Smith-Waterman as used by HaplotypeCaller

- **GATK's own aligner** (`SmithWatermanJavaAligner`, `SmithWatermanAlignmentConstants`, `CigarUtils.calculateCigar`, `AlignmentUtils.createReadAlignedToRef`; verified from source). int32 score and int32 backtrack matrices of (n+1)x(m+1); exact-substring shortcut via `lastIndexOf` returning an all-M CIGAR; tie priority per cell is diagonal >= horizontal (insertion) >= vertical (deletion); backtrack stores signed gap length; `MATRIX_MIN_CUTOFF = -1e8`. Overhang strategies: INDEL starts traceback at the corner (global); SOFTCLIP takes the max over the last column (>= scanning down) then the bottom row (strict >, or tie closer to the diagonal); LEADING_INDEL scans only the last column; IGNORE absorbs overhang into the final M. Call sites: haplotype-to-reference uses NEW_SW_PARAMETERS (200,-150,-260,-11), pads both sequences with ten Ns, overhang INDEL, then trims, left-aligns indels, and rejects any alignment containing S; read-to-haplotype uses ALIGNMENT_TO_BEST_HAPLOTYPE (10,-15,-30,-5) with SOFTCLIP and composes the read->haplotype CIGAR onto the haplotype->reference CIGAR; dangling-end recovery uses STANDARD_NGS (25,-50,-110,-6). Verdict: **adopt as the specification**: reproduce these tie rules exactly or variant representations change. With |open + extend| = 271, per-cell score deltas do not fit int8, so int16 is the narrowest usable lane.

- **SW+** (Roodi & Moshovos, CIBB 2020, LNCS 12313, doi:10.1007/978-3-030-63061-4_13). Most HC SW calls are on nearly identical sequences; replacing the DP with a linear comparison when provably equivalent cut SW time up to 40% and HC time up to 10%. Verdict: **adopt in spirit**: extend GATK's exact-match shortcut to single-mismatch/single-indel cases where the outcome under GATK's tie rules is provable, backed by a differential test against the full DP.

- **Farrar striped SW** (Bioinformatics 2007), **SSW** (Zhao et al., PLoS ONE 2013), **parasail** (Daily, BMC Bioinformatics 2016; 136 GCUPS on 24 cores). Intra-task striped 8/16-bit lanes with lazy-F correction; SSW/parasail add traceback at one byte per cell. Verdict: **consider** striped int16 for the read-to-haplotype case (150 x 600) if intra-task is chosen; tie-breaking in these libraries is not GATK's, so a custom kernel is needed regardless. GKL's anti-diagonal int32 layout is the alternative; for 150-600 bp the ramp overhead is modest and direction recording is simpler.

- **ksw2 extz2** (Li, Bioinformatics 2018; https://github.com/lh3/ksw2) and **Suzuki-Kasahara difference recurrences / libgaba** (BMC Bioinformatics 2018, doi:10.1186/s12859-018-2014-8). Store per-cell differences (dH, dV, dE, dF) so lanes can be 8-bit (ksw2: 16-way SSE core loop, 32-bit for extension scores); banded with z-drop; traceback packed in a byte per band cell; exact within the band; 2.1x over the prior adaptive-band implementation. SSE only. Verdict: **consider** difference encoding at int16 (2x lane density over int32) and its band-plus-traceback storage; ksw2's tie preferences differ from GATK's and its band is heuristic unless full width.

- **Exact banding with verification** (my synthesis). For near-identical pairs under INDEL (global) overhang, compute a band of width w around the diagonal, then certify: if no boundary cell plus the maximum achievable remaining score (match value x remaining cells) can beat the in-band optimum, the band was exact; otherwise widen. Verdict: **consider / benchmark** for haplotype-to-reference; SOFTCLIP needs a different bound because starts are free.

- **Block aligner** (Liu & Steinegger, Bioinformatics 2023; Rust crate `block-aligner`, AVX2/NEON/WASM). Adaptive blocks, 16-bit lanes, prefix-scan instead of striping, 4 bits per cell traceback (2 direction + 2 gap-extend flags); heuristic with <3% error on long reads; 9-12x over parasail on proteins. Verdict: not applicable as an aligner, but **adopt** its 4-bit traceback packing and its Rust SIMD abstraction pattern.

- **WFA / WFA2-lib** (Marco-Sola et al., Bioinformatics 2021) and **BiWFA** (Bioinformatics 2023; https://github.com/smarco/WFA2-lib). Exact O(ns + s^2) gap-affine alignment for global and ends-free modes, any match score via Eizenga's transform, ties M > X > D > I (BiWFA differs but stays optimal), auto-vectorized for AVX2/NEON/SVE. The README states true local alignment (free start and end in both sequences) is not implemented. Verdict: not applicable as a drop-in: SOFTCLIP cannot be expressed, and in INDEL mode the CIGAR chosen among equal-score alignments follows a different tie order than GATK's, so haplotype CIGARs would change. Reconsider only if tie compatibility is relaxed.

- **Bit-parallel methods** (Myers 1999; BitPAl, Loving et al. 2014). Edit distance or linear gaps only. Verdict: not applicable.

- **Compact traceback**. 2 bits (linear) or 4 bits (affine) per cell is standard; a 150 x 600 matrix is 45 KB at 4 bits vs 360 KB of int32 in GKL. Verdict: **adopt** 4-bit packing into a per-thread reusable arena (no per-call allocation). Ren et al. (BMC Genomics 2019) do SW with traceback for HC on GPU; not applicable.

## 3. JVM-to-native overhead and batching

- **GKL's JNI path** (`VectorLoglessPairHMM.java`, `IntelPairHmm.cc`, `JavaData.h`). One native call per assembly region carrying arrays of `ReadDataHolder` / `HaplotypeDataHolder` objects; the native side then does `GetObjectField` plus `GetByteArrayElements` for bases, quals, insertion GOP, deletion GOP and GCP per read and once per haplotype, releasing with mode 0. HotSpot's `Get<Prim>ArrayElements` always copies, on top of the Java-side `getBases()` copies. Per-call cost (~50-100 ns) is irrelevant; per-read JNI traffic of ~1-2 us only matters for small regions where compute is sub-millisecond. Verdict: **adopt** a flat-buffer ABI: Java packs all reads into one `byte[]` (bases, quals, GOPs, GCP, offsets) and all haplotypes into another, so the native side pins at most three arrays per call; keep one call per region.

- **Foreign Function & Memory API** (JEP 454, final in JDK 22; preview in 21; incubator in 17). Glavo's JMH suite (JDK 21; https://github.com/Glavo/java-ffi-benchmark): plain downcalls ~13% faster than JNI, 1.6x with `Linker.Option.isTrivial()`, upcalls 3.5-4x faster, struct passing ~15% slower unless allocation is hoisted; JDK 24 closed the gap further. GATK officially requires Java 17 (wiki "Switching to Java 17"), so FFM is not usable in GATK today. Verdict: **adopt** an `extern "C"` ABI over plain pointers and lengths so a JNI shim serves GATK now and an FFM binding (off-heap `MemorySegment` arena, zero-copy) replaces it when GATK moves past Java 21; do not design around JNI critical-array semantics.

- **Threading**. GKL runs OpenMP over pairs with `--native-pair-hmm-threads` (default 4) inside a single-threaded HC process; Wertenbroek & Thoma found the remaining Java stages bound end-to-end speedup once PairHMM is fast. Verdict: expose a thread-count knob, schedule (haplotype, read-batch) work items dynamically ordered by predicted cost, and expect end-to-end HC gains to saturate well below kernel gains.

## Ranked ideas worth adopting or benchmarking

1. Inter-task lanes (one read per lane against one haplotype) with batches binned by read length so no lane is masked (Endeavor's 2.14x over GKL AVX-512 is the floor; Rovaca and gpuPairHMM bin the same way).
2. Exact prefix sharing across sorted haplotypes via DP-column snapshots at LCP boundaries (GATK Java `hapStartIndex` precedent; measure LCP/length on real regions to size the gain).
3. Per-lane power-of-two row rescaling with an integer exponent lane, removing the f64 recompute (Rabiner/HMMER3); retain f64 for parity tests.
4. Per-read tables computed once per batch: 5 x n emission table indexed by haplotype base (gpuPairHMM) and transition table (GATK `recacheReadValues`), stored SoA per lane group.
5. SW: reproduce GATK's tie rules and overhang modes exactly, then add provably-equivalent fast paths for identical / single-edit sequences (SW+, GATK's `lastIndexOf` shortcut).
6. SW: int16 lanes (difference-encoded or striped), 4-bit packed traceback, per-thread arena instead of GKL's per-call int32 matrices.
7. SW: verified-exact banding for the haplotype-to-reference global case.
8. Flat-buffer `extern "C"` ABI, one call per region, JNI now and FFM later.
9. Bound-based early exit exploiting GATK's 10^-4.5 per-read likelihood cap (benchmark only; needs a sound bound and best-first ordering).
10. Include PDHMM in scope; DRAGEN-mode HC spends 75-85% of its time there.

## Examined and rejected

- Log-space PairHMM: linear space with multiply-add is why SIMD works at all.
- fp16/bf16: inadequate range (fp16) or mantissa (bf16) across ~10^5 cells; no precedent.
- Banded or pruned PairHMM as an approximation (Chen 2019; Michigan ASIC without its bound check): breaks exact equivalence.
- Endeavor's D-elimination on CPU: solves a dependency our layout lacks, at the cost of a per-row scan.
- Intra-task anti-diagonal PairHMM (GKL): lane ramp waste and cross-lane shifts on 150-row matrices; revisit only if 128-bit NEON cannot hide latency with lane groups.
- Reversed-orientation suffix sharing: valid but changes summation order, so not bit-identical; optional experiment.
- WFA/BiWFA as an SW replacement: no local (SOFTCLIP) mode and different tie order.
- Block aligner, libgaba adaptive bands, ksw2 z-drop: heuristic unless disabled, which removes their speed advantage.
- Bit-parallel alignment: no affine gaps.
- GPU/FPGA/ASIC designs: out of scope; 2017-2019 FPGAs did not beat GKL on a two-socket CPU on the 10s dataset.
- Sentieon, DRAGEN, Parabricks: closed; nothing technical published to learn from.
- `gkl` Rust crate and gatk-rs: a C++ binding and an unvalidated alpha; neither reusable.
